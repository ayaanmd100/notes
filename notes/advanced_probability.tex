1. Binomial probability

$$ \Pr(X=k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k} $$

2. Coin: exactly 5 heads in 10 flips

$$ \Pr(X=5) = \binom{10}{5} \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{10} = \frac{252}{1024} = \frac{63}{256} $$

3. Quiz: 10 true/false questions, exactly 7 correct

$$ \Pr(X=7) = \binom{10}{7} \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{10} = \frac{120}{1024} = \frac{15}{128} $$

4. Conditional probability

$$ \Pr(A | B) = \frac{\Pr(A \cap B)}{\Pr(B)} $$

5. Turn-based game (Molly p=3/5 vs Connie q=3/4, Molly first)

$$ \Pr(Molly \; wins) = \frac{p}{1-(1-p)(1-q)} = \frac{3/5}{1-(2/5)(1/4)} = \frac{3/5}{9/10} = \frac{2}{3} $$

6. Number of subsets of n-element set: $2^n$

For $S = \{A,L,G,T,W,O\}$: $2^6 = 64$ subsets.

7. Probability $4 + k^2$ is prime, k chosen from {3,5,7,11,13,17}:

Prime when k=3 (13), k=5 (29), k=7 (53), k=13 (173), k=17 (293). k=11 gives 125 = $5^3$, not prime.

$$ \Pr = \frac{5}{6} $$
