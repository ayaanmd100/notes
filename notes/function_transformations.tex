1. General form: $g(x) = a \cdot f\!\left(\frac{1}{b}(x-h)\right) + k$

2. Effect of each parameter:
- $a < 0$: reflect over x-axis (max becomes min)
- $|a| > 1$: vertical stretch
- $b > 1$: horizontal stretch
- $h$: shift right by h
- $k$: shift up by k

3. Key-point mapping: $(x_0, y_0)$ on f maps to $(x_1, y_1)$ on g

$$ x_1 = b \cdot x_0 + h, \quad y_1 = a \cdot y_0 + k $$

4. Example: $g(x) = -3f\!\left(\frac{1}{2}(x-6)\right) + 1$, so $a=-3$, $b=2$, $h=6$, $k=1$.

f has max at (-2, 5):

$$ x_1 = 2(-2)+6 = 2, \quad y_1 = -3(5)+1 = -14 \quad (min \; of \; g, \; since \; a<0) $$

f has min at (4, -6):

$$ x_1 = 2(4)+6 = 14, \quad y_1 = -3(-6)+1 = 19 \quad (max \; of \; g) $$

So w = 19.

5. Domain and range shifts for $f(x - h) + k$: domain shifts right h, range shifts up k.

f on [-2,5] with range [-1,7]: $f(x-2)+1$ has domain [0,7], range [0,8].

$$ a+b+c+d = 0+7+0+8 = 15 $$
